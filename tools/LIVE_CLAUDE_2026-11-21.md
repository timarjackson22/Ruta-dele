## 7. An English request during Spanish practice (claude-sonnet-5-5)

**Profe:** ¡Hola, Timar! Soy Profe, tu profesora de español. ¡Qué alegría conocerte! ¿De qué quieres hablar hoy?

**Learner:** can you explain the preterite in English please

**Profe:** Sure! The preterite is the past tense for things that are finished and complete: something that happened at a specific time, a set number of times, or in a sequence. For example, Ayer comí arroz con pollo means Yesterday I ate chicken and rice. " Now you try, in ¿Qué comiste ayer?

**Learner:** vale, ayer comí arroz con pollo

**Profe:** ¡Muy bien! Ayer comí arroz con pollo is correct. You used comí for a finished action yesterday. Small note: vale, okay, is very common in Spain. In Panama you'll more often hear okay, dale or listo, but vale isn't wrong. Here's the pattern for -er verbs like comer in the preterite:. yo, comí. tú, comiste. él/ella, comió. ustedes, comieron. Now let's check you can use it on your own, with a different verb. Beber, to drink, works the same way as comer. Try answering: ¿Y qué bebiste ayer?
