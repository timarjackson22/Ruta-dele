# Meaning in context brief — Verbatim and word cards follow the sentence

**Problem:** Verbatim took a word's first dictionary meaning. *Pegadita* (from *pegar*: to stick, to glue, to hit) came out as "little stuck" in a dance song, where it means dancing close. Many words change meaning with context.

**Blind spots to cover (not just pegadita)**
1. Words with several meanings: pegar (stick / hit / be close), rico (tasty / rich / hot), tiempo (time / weather), partido (match / political party), cuenta (bill / account / *darse cuenta* = realise), copa (glass / trophy), banco (bank / bench), vela (candle / sail).
2. The same word by region: guagua (bus in the Caribbean, baby in Chile), coger (to take in Spain, vulgar in much of Latin America), pelado (kid in Panama/Colombia, broke, bald).
3. Song and street language: metaphor and slang win over the literal meaning (duro = all out, fuego = hot, rico = sexy).
4. Expressions that only make sense together (darse cuenta, echar de menos, a lo mejor, tener ganas).
5. Word class by position: the same spelling as noun, adjective or verb (bajo, cocina, vino).
6. Small/coined forms keep the in-context meaning of their base (pegaditos → close together, not "little stuck").
7. Names stay names; little words (de, el) still skip ahead.
8. No context available: show the most common meaning, and the card says the meaning depends on context.

**How**
- **Context rules** for common tricky words and expressions, looking at the words around them (the sentence plus the lines before and after, so a dance mentioned in the previous line counts): pegado/pegadito + dancing or bodies → "close together"; rico + food → "tasty", + body/dancing → "hot (sexy)", + money → "rich"; tiempo + weather words → "weather"; partido + football → "match", + politics → "party"; cuenta + paying → "bill", darse cuenta → "realise"; guagua + baby words → "baby"; pelado + money → "broke", + hair → "bald"; coger + transport → "take, catch"; copa + league/final → "trophy"; vela + birthday → "candle"; and more.
- **The whole sentence's English**: every possible meaning of a word (dictionary senses, the base word's senses for coined forms) is checked against the English translation of the whole sentence; the meaning that appears there is used, in the sentence's own words. Videos now translate the sentences ahead in the background (offline translator on Android, the channel's English captions when they exist), even with English lines hidden, so Verbatim always has context. Reading and Listening already work this way; tapped words there also use the context rules.
- **Order:** context rule → meaning confirmed by the sentence → name → informal list → dictionary / word breakdown.
- **Word card:** "Here: close together" first (with "from the sentence" or "from the words around it"), then the other meanings, so you see the word has several.
- Verbatim still shows only the translation.

**Done when** tests cover a dance line (pegaditos → close together), the same word elsewhere (pegadito a la caja → stuck), rico in food vs dance lines, tiempo resolved from the sentence's English (weather), a card showing "Here" plus other meanings, and earlier tests pass.
