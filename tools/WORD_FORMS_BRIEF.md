# Word forms brief — recognise new words by breaking them down

**Problem:** songs and everyday Spanish coin words on the fly — *abracito, cafecito, poquito, cerquita, chiquitito, solecito, amorcito, bonitísimo, cucharón* — that no dictionary lists. They showed up as gaps: no Verbatim bubble, "not in the dictionary" on the word card.

**Skill: break a word into a known word + an ending, then translate the whole.**
- Endings for small / dear (diminutives): -ito/-ita(-s), -cito/-cita, -ecito/-ecita, -itito (extra small), -ico/-ica (Caribbean, Colombia, Costa Rica), -illo/-illa, -ín/-ina.
- Endings for big / intense (augmentatives): -ón/-ona, -ote/-ota, -azo/-aza. Very (superlative): -ísimo/-ísima.
- Spelling changes are undone before looking the word up: qu → c (poquito → poco), gu → g (amiguito → amigo), c → z (abracito → abrazo, lucecita → luz), lost accents come back (cafecito → café), plurals kept (abracitos → little hugs).
- When several breakdowns fit, the longest known word wins (bonitito → bonito, not bono). Words the dictionary already knows are never broken down (bonito, cita).
- Translation: a thing → "little …" (abracito → little hug); a description → "nice and …" (calentito → nice and warm); -ísimo → "very …"; big endings → "big …" / "huge …". Common fixed ones have their natural English: poquito → a little bit, ahorita → right now, cerquita → really close, chiquito → tiny, despacito → nice and slow, ratito → a little while, amorcito → sweetheart, besito → little kiss, perrito → puppy, gatito → kitten, abuelita → grandma.
- Dictionary entries that only say "diminutive of X" are turned into a real translation the same way.

**Where it shows**
- **Verbatim** (videos, Reading, Listening): the translation only (*little hug*), never a label such as "diminutive".
- **Word card:** the translation plus the breakdown, so the pattern is learned: "abrazo (hug) + -ito: small or affectionate".
- **Word check on new videos:** words solved this way go into Words › Informal straight away (no translator needed); only words that still can't be broken down are sent to the translator.

**Done when** a test list of coined words (including Bonito, Bonito-style diminutives) is translated correctly, nothing the dictionary knows is broken down, Verbatim shows no labels, and earlier tests pass.
