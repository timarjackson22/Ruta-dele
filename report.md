**Learner:** What is the difference between por and para?

**Profe:** Por vs. para (Por y para)Both words often come out as 'for' in English, but they face in opposite directions.1. Por: cause and motive — Por introduces the reason behind an action or feeling: because of, out of, on account of.e.g. Cerraron la carretera por un derrumbe. — They closed the road because of a landslide.2. Por: route, movement and vague place — Por describes movement through, along, around or past a place, and imprecise location (around here, somewhere near).e.g. Dimos un paseo por la Cinta Costera. — We took a walk along the Cinta Costera.3. Por: means and channels — Por names the medium of communication or transport: by phone, by email, by air.e.g. Te mando el contrato por correo electrónico. — I'll send you the contract by email.4. Por: exchange, price and substitution — Por marks what is given in exchange and the person or thing replaced: for, in exchange for, in place of, standing in for.e.g. Pagué doce dólares por estas sandalias. — I paid twelve dollars for these sandals.5. Por: time span, part of the day, rate — Por expresses how long something lasts, the part of the day, and rates such as 'per' and 'percent'.e.g. Viví en David por cuatro años. — I lived in David for four years.6. Por: agent, feelings, 'go and get', 'still to do' — Por introduces the doer in the passive, the object of a feeling, the thing you go to fetch, and what remains to be done.e.g. El puente fue diseñado por un ingeniero francés. — The bridge was designed by a French engineer.…6 more in the full guide (Grammar guides › Por vs. para).Say quiz me and I’ll quiz you on it, one question at a time.

## preterite-vs-imperfect (b1)

**Learner:** When do I use imperfect vs preterite

**Profe:** Preterite vs. imperfect (Pretérito e imperfecto)Spanish has two simple past tenses, and choosing between them is about viewpoint, not about how long something lasted.1. Preterite: completed, bounded events — Use the preterite for an action viewed as finished at a specific point or within a defined period.e.g. Ayer compré un ventilador nuevo. — Yesterday I bought a new fan.2. Preterite: a chain of events — When actions happen one after another and move the story forward, each one is preterite.e.g. Me levanté, me bañé y salí corriendo. — I got up, showered and ran out.3. Preterite: counted times and measured periods — A precise number of repetitions or a period with clear limits makes the action a closed block, so preterite.e.g. Fui al dentista tres veces el mes pasado. — I went to the dentist three times last month.4. Preterite: beginnings, endings and change — Starting, finishing and sudden changes are points in time, so verbs that express them are usually preterite.e.g. La película empezó a las ocho. — The movie started at eight.5. Imperfect: habits and repeated actions — Use the imperfect for what used to happen or would happen regularly, with no count.e.g. De niño, jugaba béisbol todas las tardes. — As a kid, I used to play baseball every afternoon.6. Imperfect: description and background — Use the imperfect to set the scene: time, date, age, weather, appearance, physical and mental states.e.g. Eran las once y hacía un calor terrible. — It was eleven o'clock and it was terribly hot.…6 more in the full guide (Grammar guides › Preterite vs. imperfect).Say quiz me and I’ll quiz you on it, one question at a time.
